# A public deliverable as evidence about expert work

[Cure53's September 2024 Obsidian Sync API/server/crypto report](https://github.com/cure53/Publications/blob/3070b31cf51e966d504303936f0a12f20b198911/pentest-report_obsidian-3.pdf) was inspected through complete extracted pages 1–4, 14 and 16 of 16. [Provenance](sample-report-pin.json) records bytes, hash and pinned repository commit. No private source or disclosed secret was used.

Page 2 reports eight invested days and five senior testers, spanning preparation, execution, documentation and delivery; it does not give per-person or phase-level hours. Multiplying those numbers into forty person-days would be unjustified. Pages 2–4 distinguish work packages, source/documentation access, coordination and explicit commits. This illustrates access and domain expertise shaping an engagement, not representative costs.

Page 14 retains a finding explicitly marked false positive after developer clarification. This shows why adjudication and correction history matter. The surrounding runtime-validation rationale deserves independent technical review; the label does not make every sentence authoritative. Pages 3 and 16 identify missing design specifications as uncertainty. That matters when establishing a correctness oracle.

Only selected pages were reviewed. No remediation retest was established. Rendering tools were unavailable, so this was textual evidence review, not layout verification or a full cryptographic audit.
