# Technical gaps and experiments worth trying

These are hypotheses, not a startup pitch or a recommendation to automate the assessor. Each needs an experiment that can fail. The current evidence supports narrower mechanisms than a universal autonomous tester.

| Approach and mechanism | Evidence so far | Required expertise/access | Smallest credible experiment | Result that would disprove usefulness |

## Follow the cost, including shifted work

For each experiment record access/setup time, investigation, false-positive validation, domain clarification, report preparation, review, fix coordination and retest. Keep analyst and machine time separate. Report model calls, tokens and billed cost when used, including failed calls. The current lab has no metered API use but that does not make engineering work free. Count verified useful outcomes, missed known cases, benign functionality and unintended effects, rather than just task throughput.

An assistant may be most useful at keeping hypotheses, evidence and limits consistent while an expert chooses the important question. That is a hypothesis about collaboration, not evidence that full autonomy is desirable. A careful manual workflow is an essential baseline.

## Connection to NBRET

NBRET's capture and assertions are useful ingredients; the research does not authorize or implement a new production schema. Tomo's [follow-up plan](../security-landscape/lab-followup-plan.md) and receipt at `runs/security-lab-1792263876973/receipt.json` identify a concrete boundary: matching method, URL and body did not bind the saved observation to the requested synthetic identity. Agate read that plan and receipt; the source-level finding and live lab remain Tomo's work. This is evidence-attribution ambiguity, not an authorization exploit against a live application.

The next engineering experiment should establish identity/environment equivalence before reusing an observation, while avoiding storage of raw credentials in durable claim keys. A pseudonymous principal plus authoritative role/tenant and session-policy epoch may help, but its privacy, revocation and collision behavior must be tested. Automatically hashing every header is not a sufficient design: rotating secrets, redaction and semantically relevant non-identity headers need explicit treatment.

These observations justify discriminating experiments, not adoption decisions. The OpenFGA local tests establish a narrow mechanism; the benchmark fixtures establish selected scorer semantics; neither supplies incident prevention rates, field economics or longitudinal remediation outcomes. The monitor's rejected OS hypothesis also illustrates why negative and contradictory results belong in the knowledge store: they can prevent repeating a plausible but wrong inference.

OpenFGA's next dependency layer adds two concrete limits: actual internal store-descriptor deletion retains tuple/model/assertion maps, and actual condition compilation loses its first error on repeated Compile before a caught evaluation panic. Neither establishes public deleted-data access or a production crash path. They motivate **data-class-specific deletion receipts** and **persistent error-state contracts**. The smallest experiments respectively verify every retained copy after a synthetic deletion, and mutation-test repeated failing compilation through the actual validation/reuse caller. The proposals fail if residual data remains available beyond the stated retention contract, or a failed evaluation is promoted into a clean denial/success instead of an explicit error.
