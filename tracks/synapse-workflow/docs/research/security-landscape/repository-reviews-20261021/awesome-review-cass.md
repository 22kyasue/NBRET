At the inspected revision it has five tracked files: README, contribution guide, CC0 license, a decorative banner and an issue/thread maintenance workflow.

All four tracked text files were read. The banner was inventoried, not analyzed visually; no visual claim depends on it. Linked repositories and websites are separate research scopes; none of their implementation claims is admitted merely by being listed here.

## What it tells us about the research lab

The useful separation is **knowledge catalog → reviewed method → admitted experiment → observed result**. Awesome-Hacking supplies the first layer. Our existing research register supplies a stronger second layer for claims already studied: exact studies, evidence strengths, corrections, hazards and versioned source snapshots. Our experiment/effect/adjudication layers remain separate.

A useful lab must not treat a tool's appearance on an awesome list as permission to execute it, as a guarantee of installed capability or as a proven finding method. Reading more catalogs alone does not improve discovery; a candidate method needs an experiment and a way it can be disproved.

This directory also shows why the platform should be extensible by target family rather than hard-coded to one giant commerce app. The catalog spans more disciplines than our first investigation can responsibly cover. We can retain that breadth in the knowledge model while admitting one bounded method/target/effect contract at a time.

## Proposed knowledge/method record

For a reviewed method, add fields the upstream directory does not provide: source/version/license evidence; target family and access model; hypothesis and authoritative rule; prerequisites; permitted effects and budget; required tools/version; positive and secure controls; exact result/evidence contract; evaluator/version; retained failure/unknown states; applicable safety hazards; prior experiments; exposure/contamination; and a retirement/review trigger.

Keep the original catalog row immutable as provenance while interpretations/reviews evolve. A later broken link, tool upgrade or rejected hypothesis should become a dated status, not silently disappear. The model may search/propose a method; execution is separately admitted outside model choice. These are design applications of our existing evidence/authorization research, not features supplied by Awesome-Hacking.

## Research and decision record

Decision: incorporate this project as a provenance-preserving candidate knowledge index, not an engine, benchmark or bulk installation plan. Alternatives: keep only external links, or build a curated internal method catalog. The JSON provides a lightweight starting point without runtime integration; existing studies can gradually replace unknowns with reviewed method cards.

Evidence strength: repository structure and README/workflow/contribution content are source/documentation reading at a pin; no runtime/security-effect evidence. Relevant existing register entries: `ai-evaluation.audit-evaluator-before-tester` (framework.md:79–91; draft synthesis), `commercial.evaluation-and-buyer-evidence-protocol` (synthesis.md:72–74; synthesis), and `service-delivery.rea-integration-order` (October 16 handoff; proposed adoption sequence, with explicit no-runtime limit). These support evaluating a specific question with existing tools before adding another adapter.

Contrary limits: the list can be useful for human discovery even without machine schemas; it does not need to be an execution platform to have value. Inclusion still does not settle maintenance, child implementation quality, cost, rights or safe operation. A method catalog adds maintenance cost; more entries may worsen retrieval or distract investigation.

Validation: all five file hashes and 81 extracted rows/line references are retained. A useful follow-up would compare selecting relevant reviewed methods with the internal catalog versus the existing register/manual links on matched questions, including irrelevant/stale entries and total review time. No such experiment was performed. If retrieval quality or useful confirmed work does not improve after maintenance effort, the catalog integration hypothesis fails.
