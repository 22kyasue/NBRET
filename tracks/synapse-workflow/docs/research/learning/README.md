# Security, understood: the learning reader

Open [Inside cybersecurity, the offline research publication](index.html). It contains seven substantial chapters, eleven company dossiers with full supporting studies, implementation notebooks, a runnable local lab and the complete searchable document snapshot. The eight short visual introductions remain optional.

The current publication covers how attacks work, services and delivery, technical tools, testing engines, defensive operations, AI/worker systems and reproduction. Each company dossier connects its business and technical work to complete canonical studies. Build blueprints and the worked service exercise are editorial designs, clearly separate from observed proprietary behavior. See [publication structure and verification](PUBLICATION.md).

The opening overview summarizes all five tracks, their concrete takeaways and remaining limits, with a suggested 20-minute reading path. Expand a track to see what was researched and follow its canonical studies. The snapshot also includes the earlier REA research handoff. Counts include research plans and continuation documents; they are not counts of validated discoveries.

The introductory topic synthesis is written for Kotaro's learning goals. Canonical research remains in the five source directories. The reader does not replace detailed annotations or merge their different evidence levels into an assurance claim.

## Continuing a conversation

Refer to a topic ID, such as “05: worker leases and cancellation,” or copy a topic/article discussion prompt. Topic notes are browser-local and must be copied or exported to carry them into another chat or browser. Future sessions should start with [CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md).

## Refreshing

Run `node scripts/build-learning-reader.mjs` from NBRET root. The builder verifies all curated topic-source paths, embeds Markdown as escaped data, and writes `build-receipt.json` with per-document hashes and a snapshot timestamp. It does not edit the source studies. Active research may be newer than the HTML snapshot.
